NYMEHow I designed it.

I designed and built NYME’s website to explain a job application service: what candidates receive, how the work is checked and who controls submission. Follow the site in the window, with my design decisions beside each section.

Design process · 1 of 8

NYMEHow I designed it.

Product design, research and build. Solo, 2025–26.

Problem
I need to explain three application formats while making the service’s purpose clear.
Decision in this section
A promise about speed also needs to explain what the candidate receives. I lead with readiness for a new role and name the CV, letter and website beneath it.

The package · 2 of 8

02

Give each format a job.

Starting point
I have three formats to explain within one application.
What I saw
The CV makes relevant experience easy to find. The letter explains the fit. The website gives the candidate’s proposed contribution more room.
Decision
I group them in one switcher, with a distinct purpose for each.

Timing · 3 of 8

03

Explain when the work starts.

Starting point
I make readiness part of the opening promise.
What I saw
The headline makes a claim about speed; the page needs to explain its basis.
Decision
I place the timing section before the detailed process and show preparation alongside an example application timeline.

The example · 4 of 8

04

Let the reader inspect the work.

Starting point
I’m asking the reader to understand an application package.
What I saw
Describing the formats leaves their relationship abstract. A shared role gives the reader something concrete to examine.
Decision
I show a CV, letter and website for one role, clearly labelled as a fictional example.

The process · 5 of 8

05

Make the checks visible.

Starting point
I’m explaining the work behind the finished package.
What I saw
A polished example leaves questions about missing evidence, corrections and permission to send.
Decision
I show six steps from finding a role to recording the outcome, including the checks before submission.

Submission · 6 of 8

06

Show who sends.

Starting point
I offer two ways to use the service.
What I saw
Preparing an application and submitting it are separate responsibilities. The difference needs to be explicit.
Decision
I distinguish candidate submission from an invitation-only service with a named sender and written permission.

Access · 7 of 8

07

Finish with the candidate’s control.

Starting point
I’m closing a page about preparing applications on someone’s behalf.
What I saw
Requesting access and authorising a submission are separate decisions.
Decision
I end with limited access, approval boundaries and direct answers about what can be sent.

Result · 8 of 8

08

The page shows the package, its checks and who controls submission.

Result
Next, I would test whether candidates can explain who sends in each service.
