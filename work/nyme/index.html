NYMEHow I designed it.

How do you make an application service easy to understand while keeping candidates in control? I designed and built NYME’s website around three questions: what you receive, how it is checked and who can send it. This case follows the design decisions and what needs testing next.

The design challenge · 1 of 8

NYMEHow I designed it.

Audience
Candidates who want a considered application for a relevant role, but have limited time to prepare it.
Design challenge
Explain what they can delegate without making them uncertain about what happens in their name.
Decision
I introduce the outcome first, then name the three formats and make candidate control part of the opening message.

Product design, content and build. Solo, 2025–26.

Information hierarchy · 2 of 8

Give each format a clear purpose.

Design challenge
Three formats can look like three versions of the same information.
Design reasoning
Showing one format at a time gives each room, but makes comparison less immediate. Clear labels need to keep the whole package understandable.
Decision
I use one switcher: the CV surfaces relevant experience, the letter explains the fit, and the website develops the proposed contribution.

One application package, with a clear role for each format.

Setting expectations · 3 of 8

Explain the promise of readiness.

Design challenge
The opening promise depends on work that happens before the candidate starts writing.
Design reasoning
A timeline makes the sequence concrete, but precise dates can also imply a guaranteed result.
Decision
I place preparation before the detailed process. In testing, I would check whether readers distinguish the example timeline from a delivery promise.

Make the sequence clear without overstating the outcome.

Making it concrete · 4 of 8

Let the reader inspect the work.

Design challenge
Descriptions alone leave the relationship between the formats abstract.
Design reasoning
Using the same candidate and role lets readers compare how each format answers the same need.
Decision
I show an inspectable CV, letter and website for one role, clearly labelled as a fictional example.

One candidate. One role. Three connected formats.

Process transparency · 5 of 8

Make the checks visible.

Design challenge
A polished example does not explain how unsupported claims or missing information are handled.
Design reasoning
Visible stage headings provide an overview. Expandable details explain exceptions without asking every reader to work through the full process.
Decision
I show six stages with expandable explanations, including evidence gaps, corrections and checks before submission.

Explain the exceptions as clearly as the intended path.

User control · 6 of 8

Make responsibility explicit.

Design challenge
Preparing an application and sending it are different responsibilities.
Design reasoning
Candidates need to compare the services by who acts on their behalf, not only by what is included.
Decision
I place the two services side by side and name the sender in each: the candidate, or a named person acting within written permission.

Who sends is a core service choice.

Trust and access · 7 of 8

Address uncertainty before action.

Design challenge
Requesting access should not be confused with permission to submit an application.
Design reasoning
Questions about fit, inaccurate information and sending deserve direct answers near the access request.
Decision
I close with approval boundaries and an FAQ that explains what happens when information is missing or disputed.

Make the limits visible where the candidate decides.

Validation · 8 of 8

Test understanding, not just appearance.

What the design provides
An inspectable package, an explanation of its checks and an explicit distinction between senders.
What needs testing
Whether candidates can explain the service, understand the timing claim and identify what requires their approval.
Next test
Ask candidates to compare the formats and explain who sends in each service. Observe where they hesitate or draw the wrong conclusion.

The next test is whether candidates can explain their choices.
